Era 01

The Ancient world

3000 – 500 BCE

The first cities appeared in four river valleys simultaneously — the Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, Indus, and Yellow River. Writing was invented to keep records. Kings claimed divinity. The great questions were: how do we feed a city, and how do we hold it together?

The Classical era

500 BCE – 500 CE

The Axial Age reset humanity's moral and intellectual compass. Greek democracy, Roman law, Buddhist compassion, and Confucian ethics all emerged within a few centuries. Empires spanned continents. The first world-system of trade — the Silk Road — began to connect them.

Era 03

The Medieval era

500 – 1400 CE

The fall of Rome did not darken the world — it reshuffled it. Byzantium preserved Greek learning. The Islamic Golden Age advanced mathematics, astronomy, and medicine. Tang China was the most cosmopolitan place on earth. The Mongols briefly unified Eurasia, enabling trade on a scale never seen before.

The Early modern era

Europe's ships connected the world's hemispheres for the first time, with catastrophic consequences for the Americas and Africa. The printing press spread ideas faster than power could suppress them. Gunpowder transformed warfare. The Scientific Revolution asked whether God's universe was also a machine.

About Chronos

Why map history?

History is usually taught as a series of national stories: the rise of Rome, the spread of Islam, the birth of the USA. Chronos tries something different — it maps the connections between civilisations: the trade routes, the borrowed ideas, the military shocks that rippled across continents.

The goal is not to provide every fact, but to give you a mental model of how the world's complexity accumulated. Each era built on the last. The Axial Age philosophers argued with each other across centuries. The Ottoman Empire was the Roman Empire's bureaucratic heir. The Industrial Revolution required coal, iron, and colonies — a supply chain spanning four continents.

Hover a dot, click a card, follow a thread. The deeper you go, the more connected it becomes.
